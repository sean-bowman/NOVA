
# Thrust-Optimized Contour References

Sources gathered to build and check NOVA's thrust-optimized contour and thrust-optimized parabola against published practice. Collected 2026-09-13.

The question driving the search: given a solver that can march a wall it is handed, what published work fixes how a maximum-thrust wall should be parametrized, what gain that parametrization is entitled to claim over the parabolic approximation, and what independent data exists for a perfect bell that the truncated ideal contour's reference set does not already supply.

These back the family taxonomy in `NozzleContourMethods.md` and the per-family checks in `NozzleContourValidation.md`. The truncated ideal contour's own sources are in `references_nozzleContour_2026-09-06.md` and are cross-referenced rather than repeated here.

## Allman and Hoffman (1981), Design of Maximum Thrust Nozzle Contours by Direct Optimization Methods

- **URL:** <http://servidor.demec.ufpr.br/CFD/bibliografia/propulsao/allman_hoffman_1981.pdf>. AIAA Journal vol. 19, no. 6, June 1981, pp. 750-751; DOI 10.2514/3.50999. Originally AIAA paper 78-1048, 14th Joint Propulsion Conference, Las Vegas, July 1978.
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-13, full text, two pages. The host serves the file over plain HTTP; the HTTPS URL fails its TLS handshake, so a fetch that upgrades the scheme returns nothing. The AIAA copy of record is paywalled.
- **Relevance:** The method NOVA's thrust-optimized contour implements, and the only source in the set that measures a directly optimized wall against a Rao contour at matched length. It is what makes the difference between the two families a number rather than an expectation.
- **Key findings:**
  - The wall is a second-degree polynomial, `y(x) = a + b x + c x^2`, attached to a fixed circular initial-expansion arc. NOVA's throat arc plus a Bezier wall is the same construction in a different algebra.
  - **There are only two independent design variables**, the attachment angle `theta_a` and the exit radius `y_e`. The three polynomial coefficients follow from those two plus the requirement that the polynomial attach to the arc continuously and with continuous wall slope. The authors state that `theta_a` and `y_e` therefore determine a unique nozzle configuration.
  - The design constraint is a specified nozzle length. The gasdynamic model is isentropic perfect-gas flow, and thrust is the sum of the pressure and momentum flux across the throat plus the integral of the pressure forces along the wall.
  - Three direct optimization methods are compared: multidimensional line search, steepest descent, and Newton's method. All three converge to the same contour, that contour is the global maximum, and each reaches it without an excessive number of base point moves. The method is chosen for efficiency rather than for finding a different answer.
  - **Table 1 is the result that matters.** Against Rao contours at thirteen matched lengths, the optimized second-degree polynomial delivers `dF` of **-0.05 to -0.21 percent at zero ambient pressure** and -0.22 to -0.66 percent at 0.340 atm. The polynomial is slightly worse than Rao in every case and better in none.
  - The authors attribute the bulk of that difference to the restriction to a second-degree wall, and state that **higher-degree polynomials could produce closer agreement and may be required at other operating conditions**. NOVA's cubic with four design variables is that recommendation taken up, and it contains the second-degree family as a surface inside it.
  - Agreement at zero ambient pressure is within 0.2 percent, which the authors identify as **the approximate precision of their own characteristics flowfield algorithm**. Any cross-family claim at or below that size is a statement about the mesh rather than about the contour, which is the same limit NOVA's own mesh sweep runs into.
  - The stated drawback of the variational approach, and the motivation for the direct one, is that changing the nozzle configuration or the gasdynamic model forces the entire optimization analysis and its program to be reworked. A direct method changes only the objective.

## NASA RP-1104, Tuttle and Blount (1983), Perfect Bell Nozzle Parametric and Optimization Curves

- **URL:** <https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19830016278/downloads/19830016278.pdf>. NASA Reference Publication 1104, Marshall Space Flight Center, May 1983, 32 pages.
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-13, full text. The scan carries no text layer, so the figures and tables were read as images rather than extracted.
- **Relevance:** An external reference for the ideal contour that the existing truncated-ideal source set does not contain. It is generated by the same method NOVA uses, under the same uniform-parallel-exit constraint, and it tabulates the one free parameter of NOVA's truncated ideal contour that nothing else checks: the design area ratio an ideal nozzle is run to before being cut.
- **Key findings:**
  - Perfect bell nozzles by method of characteristics, **gamma = 1.2**, design area ratios `A_D` from **10 to 6100**, vacuum (`PA = 0.0`). The imposed constraint is that the exit velocity vectors at the untruncated design area ratio are uniform in magnitude and parallel to the centerline. That is NOVA's ideal contour construction.
  - Three thrust coefficients are reported and they are not interchangeable. `C_fg` is the gross two-dimensional coefficient without friction. `C_fn` is net of wall shear. `C_fi` is the one-dimensional isentropic coefficient at the same area ratio.
  - **Nozzle efficiency is `ETA_n = C_fn / C_fi`.** NOVA's inviscid coefficient is comparable against `C_fg`, not against `ETA_n`, unless NOVA's own boundary layer is run at the report's friction factor. Comparing an inviscid coefficient against `ETA_n` biases the result against NOVA by the friction NOVA is not being charged for.
  - Table 1 gives the program inputs and they are the disclosure a comparison needs: **`R = 0.0`, a corner expansion**, chosen because it yields shorter nozzles than a radius downstream of the throat at a given area ratio. NOVA uses a 0.382 throat-radius exit arc, so the two throat treatments differ and the near-throat wall is not directly comparable.
  - **`FM = 1.005`, the starting line Mach number for uniform flow.** NOVA uses a Sauer transonic line, so the starting conditions differ as well.
  - **`CF1 = 0.003`**, the incompressible skin friction coefficient, described as a representative Fanning value equal to 0.012 Darcy-Weisbach. The authors note large high-pressure nozzles would run a little lower and small low-pressure nozzles a little higher.
  - **`DRAG1 = 0.011`, a thrust loss at the throat**, applied on top of the wall friction. Both debits are in `C_fn` and therefore in `ETA_n`.
  - Wall friction is applied only after the frictionless contour has been determined, so **it does not affect the contour or the surface area**. The contour is an inviscid result even though the efficiency is not.
  - The figure set: `L/D_t` against `A_D` (figures 2 to 4), contour surface area `A_s/A_t` (5 to 7), **wall angle against local area ratio (figure 8)**, wall pressure as `A_e/A_t` against `P_e/P_c` (9 and 10), `ETA_n` against `A_D` (11 to 13), and **the design area ratio and length that maximize `ETA_n` for a given exit area ratio (14 and 15)**. The last pair is the optimum-truncation set and is the strongest single check available for NOVA's truncation choice.
  - Efficiency has a maximum in `A_D` for each exit area ratio, because `C_fi` is fixed by the exit area ratio while `C_fn` is not. Extending a contour past the point where wall friction exceeds the pressure contribution reduces total thrust.
  - **Conclusion, quoted in substance:** minimum length perfect bell nozzles are similar in contour and performance to the minimum length nozzles designed by the Rao optimization method. The actual difference in performance at a given length is stated to be very small, and the two differ because the Rao nozzles do not carry the uniform-parallel-flow constraint at the untruncated design area ratio.
  - Contours at `A_D` of 600 and above are noted as not shown to completion, with erratic values attributed to the convergence tolerance as the code iterated to the requested inputs. Digitizing that region inherits the disclaimer.

## Rao (1958), Exhaust Nozzle Contour for Optimum Thrust

- **URL:** Jet Propulsion vol. 28, no. 6, June 1958, pp. 377-382. Recorded in full in `references_nozzleContour_2026-09-06.md`.
- **Accessed:** Not directly; the article is paywalled and remains so in this pass.
- **Relevance:** The contour that Allman and Hoffman's Table 1 measures against, and therefore the standard NOVA's optimized wall is indirectly being held to. Listed here as the target of the comparison rather than as a method NOVA implements.
- **Key findings:**
  - Cited by Allman and Hoffman as reference 1 and characterized there as a **one-dimensional variational problem**, which is the distinction between the two approaches: no constraint is placed on the form of the contour other than that it be a streamline, so the variational method yields the absolute best solution to the specific design problem posed.
  - That absolute-best property is why a direct method's shortfall in Table 1 is negative in every row. A parametrized family cannot beat the unconstrained optimum, so the useful question is how little it loses.

## Sadhana 46 (2021), Rocket nozzles: 75 years of research and development

- **URL:** <https://www.ias.ac.in/article/fulltext/sadh/046/0076> (Indian Academy of Sciences, open access). Khare and Saha, Sadhana vol. 46, article 76, 2021.
- **Accessed:** 2026-09-06 during the survey that seeded this work. **Not re-opened on 2026-09-13**: the full-text URL returned HTTP 403 in this pass, so the two figures below carry the confidence of the earlier reading and were not re-confirmed against the article.
- **Relevance:** The review that supplies the expected magnitude of a length-constrained optimization gain and the length relationship between a truncated ideal contour and a parabolic baseline. It is what NOVA's cross-family ordering is measured against, which is why its provenance matters.
- **Key findings:**
  - Length-constrained optimization is reported to buy on the order of 0.5 to 1 percent in thrust at equal length.
  - A truncated ideal contour at the same area ratio as a parabolic baseline delivers nearly identical thrust while running roughly 10 percent longer. The penalty shows up as length rather than as performance.
  - **Both figures should be re-checked against the article before either is quoted as a validation criterion.** They are currently the weakest-provenance numbers in this file, and they are the ones the cross-family ordering is scored against.

## What these sources support and what they do not

**Supported.** The parametrization of a directly optimized wall, from the paper that introduced it, including the fact that its two design variables follow from tangential attachment to a fixed initial arc. A measured comparison between a directly optimized polynomial and a Rao contour at matched length, with a sign and a magnitude. A stated precision floor for a characteristics-based cross-family comparison, from authors who hit it. An external, independently generated perfect bell data set at a stated gamma, with its throat treatment, starting line, friction model and efficiency definition all disclosed, and with the optimum truncation tabulated.

**Not supported.** A published wall table for an optimized contour, at any area ratio, from any source in this set. Both Allman and Hoffman and RP-1104 publish performance and figures rather than coordinates, so a node-by-node comparison remains impossible for the optimized families exactly as it is for the truncated ideal one. Rao's variational conditions in their original form, since Rao (1958) is paywalled and Zucrow and Hoffman was not opened in this pass. And any optimized-contour data at a gamma near NOVA's worked LOX/LH2 case, since RP-1104 fixes gamma at 1.2.

**One consequence for how NOVA's own result has to be read.** Allman and Hoffman optimize a quadratic wall and land 0.05 to 0.21 percent below Rao. NOVA optimizes a cubic and lands above the parabola it started from. Those are not in conflict, because NOVA's baseline is a parabola read off a digitized chart rather than an optimized quadratic, and the chart carries a recorded transcription error and an extrapolated region. Separating the two requires optimizing over the quadratic subfamily, which the cubic parametrization contains exactly, and comparing that against both the chart parabola and the cubic optimum. Until that is run, part of NOVA's measured gain is attributable to the chart rather than to the family.
